# busd on a Ryzen 7 7840U, with the benchmark on zbus 6

Measured on 2026-10-20 on the laptop of [busd against dbus-broker and dbus-daemon, on a Ryzen 7
7840U](2026-10-18-busd-zruntime-ryzen-7840u.md): an AMD Ryzen 7 7840U (8 cores, 16 threads),
running Fedora 44 with Linux 7.1.13, through
[`scripts/compare-busd-builds.sh`][busd-builds], with the energy counters of
the CPU package open to it. The client is the benchmark on zbus 6, which
[the benchmark on zbus 6](2026-10-20-zbus-6-xeon-4vcpu.md) measured on the VM, built from the main
branch at cd5c620 (the results do not name the commit: the script records it only since). The
builds of busd and zruntime are those of [zruntime's busy polling on a Ryzen 7
7840U](2026-10-19-zruntime-busy-poll-ryzen-7840u.md), built with rustc 1.99.0, with busypoll-off
and busypoll20 against z-galaxy/zruntime#52 at d91ca1b. busd-zruntime is the port of busd to
zruntime at 0d22862, which builds the same code as busd's main branch at a982812, and busd-tokio
is busd at 840d728, the last commit of that branch on Tokio. dbus-broker 37 and dbus-daemon 1.16.2
are those that Fedora 44 ships.

## Summary

* With the benchmark on zbus 6, every broker but dbus-daemon gets through the 20-client run in
  about half the time that it took with the benchmark on zbus 4.3, on 2026-10-18: dbus-broker in
  0.451 s against 0.930 s, busd on zruntime in 0.475 s against 0.926 s, and busd on Tokio in
  0.468 s against 0.917 s. dbus-daemon, which limits that run, takes as long as it did, 1.195 s
  against 1.185 s (p = 0.10). With a single client, the brokers are 11 to 29% faster. Those runs
  were not interleaved with this one, so these differences mix the client's move to zbus 6 with
  whatever else changed from one day to the next, which their size dwarfs.
* Now that the client is faster, dbus-broker comes out ahead of busd on zruntime: busd takes 5.3%
  longer with 20 clients and 13% longer with a single client, and spends 19% more CPU time in the
  20-client phase, 0.37 s against 0.31 s (all p < 0.0001). On zbus 4.3, the two were level with
  20 clients (p = 0.46). busd still needs 21% less memory: 6.4 MiB against 8.1 MiB (p < 0.0001).
* Against busd on Tokio, busd on zruntime spends 54% less CPU time in the 20-client phase, 0.37 s
  against 0.80 s (p < 0.0001), is 6.2% faster with a single client (p = 0.0002) and 1.5% slower
  with 20 (p = 0.03), and needs 18% less memory (p < 0.0001).
* The client is busy 95 to 97% of the 20-client phase with every broker but dbus-daemon, and 75%
  with dbus-daemon, which is busy 98% of the time. So it is still the client that limits the
  timings of all the other brokers, which come within 6% of each other there, and their CPU time
  is what tells them apart. busd on zruntime is busy 77% of that phase now, against 46% on
  zbus 4.3, and dbus-broker 68%, against 44%.
* The spin before every sleep and the busy polling now pay off with a single client, where the
  busy polling gained nothing on zbus 4.3: busd-spin20 takes 0.056 s, 22% less than busd-spin0,
  and busypoll20 0.060 s, 17% less than busypoll-off (both p < 0.0001). That puts busd-spin20 10%
  ahead of dbus-broker (p < 0.0001), and busypoll20 level with it (p = 0.17). With 20 clients, they
  gain little or nothing, 1.8% for the spin (p = 0.20) and 0.8% for the busy polling (p = 0.03),
  for 16% more CPU time each (p < 0.0001).
* On one CPU, the spin and the busy polling make the single client 52 and 53% slower (both
  p < 0.0001), against 42 and 41% on 2026-10-19.
* Under light traffic, where the benchmark's client takes no part, the CPU times and latencies
  agree with those of 2026-10-19: busypoll20 spends 10 to 13% more CPU time per call (p < 0.01),
  and spin20 44 to 103% more (p < 0.0001). This time, the energy that the CPU package used differs
  for none of the builds (p > 0.14), where busypoll20 had it use 13% more with forwarded calls on
  2026-10-19 (p = 0.005). In every test, the package used more energy than on 2026-10-19, up to
  nearly twice as much, which these runs do not explain.

## Results

Timings are medians, with the fastest and slowest run in parentheses. "Broker CPU" is the
broker's CPU time in the 20-client phase, and "busy" that over the phase's duration, summed over
the broker's threads and processes. "Client busy" is the same for the client. "Sleeps" is how many
times the broker gave up the CPU to wait (voluntary context switches) over the whole run, and
"RSS" its peak resident memory, summed over its processes. All are medians. The differences called
out come from a Mann–Whitney U test (20,000 permutations).

One warmup round and 15 measured rounds.

| busd-zruntime | 0.070 (0.066–0.075) | 0.475 (0.455–0.507) | 0.37 | 77% | 95% | 5,439 | 6.43 |

| busd-spin20 | 0.056 (0.053–0.058) | 0.463 (0.444–0.486) | 0.43 | 92% | 97% | 803 | 6.43 |
| busypoll-off | 0.072 (0.069–0.076) | 0.475 (0.460–0.506) | 0.37 | 78% | 96% | 5,525 | 6.44 |
| busypoll20 | 0.060 (0.055–0.065) | 0.471 (0.452–0.491) | 0.43 | 91% | 97% | 1,036 | 6.51 |

* busd-spin0 and busypoll-off measure as busd-zruntime does (p > 0.05), as they should.
* busypoll20 is 7% slower than busd-spin20 with a single client (p = 0.0001), and level with it
  with 20 clients and in CPU time (p > 0.3).
* dbus-daemon takes 2.3 times as long as dbus-broker with a single client and 2.6 times as long
  with 20, and spends 3.8 times its CPU time in the 20-client phase.

### On one CPU

The client and every broker on CPU 0. One warmup round and 10 measured rounds.

| Broker | 1 client, s | 20 clients, s | Broker CPU, s | Sleeps |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| busypoll-off | 0.071 (0.069–0.074) | 1.091 (1.017–1.173) | 0.47 | 17,392 |
| busypoll20 | 0.109 (0.106–0.111) | 1.111 (1.044–1.220) | 0.51 | 6,650 |
| spin20 | 0.108 (0.106–0.110) | 1.155 (1.045–1.229) | 0.54 | 6,398 |

* busypoll20 is 53% slower than busypoll-off with a single client (p < 0.0001), as fast with 20
  clients (p = 0.35), and spends 8.5% more CPU time (p = 0.0009).
* spin20 is 52% slower with a single client (p < 0.0001), 5.9% slower with 20 (p = 0.015), and
  spends 15% more CPU time (p = 0.0001). busypoll20 and spin20 differ in nothing (p > 0.07).
* Every build is faster than on 2026-10-19, busypoll-off by 22% with a single client and by 32%
  with 20 (both p < 0.0001).

### Under light traffic

10 rounds of each test, every run 3 s long, with the columns of [zruntime's busy polling on a Ryzen
7 7840U](2026-10-19-zruntime-busy-poll-ryzen-7840u.md#under-light-traffic). "CPU per call" has the
lowest and highest run in parentheses.

| Broker | Calls | CPU per call, µs | Latency, µs | p99, µs | Sleeps | Energy, J |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **To the bus, every 1 ms** | | | | | | |
| off | 3,000 | 49.3 (42.4–62.7) | 80 | 229 | 3,012 | 49.1 |
| busypoll20 | 3,000 | 54.1 (49.7–66.2) | 80 | 240 | 3,013 | 44.7 |

| busypoll50 | 3,000 | 130.3 (127.7–132.1) | 107 | 253 | 3,126 | 42.5 |
| spin20 | 3,000 | 95.6 (93.4–105.5) | 117 | 274 | 5,358 | 55.3 |
| **To the bus, every 50 µs** | | | | | | |
| off | 56,584 | 19.2 (18.7–19.4) | 38 | 91 | 56,593 | 73.4 |
| busypoll20 | 56,818 | 21.7 (21.1–22.0) | 40 | 89 | 56,801 | 70.5 |
| spin20 | 57,907 | 38.9 (38.5–39.4) | 37 | 78 | 52,902 | 71.6 |

* **To the bus, every 1 ms**: busypoll20 spends 9.9% more CPU time per call (p = 0.007), and
  spin20 44% more (p < 0.0001). Their latencies and energy differ from off's in nothing (p > 0.4).
* **Forwarded, every 1 ms**: busypoll20 spends 12% more CPU time per call (p < 0.0001), and its
  calls take 3.6% longer (p = 0.0002). busypoll50 spends 137% more, and its calls are 9% faster
  (both p < 0.0001), where on 2026-10-19 they were 10% slower (p = 0.11). spin20 spends 74% more,
  for calls that take as long (p = 0.38). The energy differs for none of them (p > 0.14).
* **To the bus, every 50 µs**: busypoll20 spends 13% more CPU time per call, and its calls take
  6.3% longer (both p < 0.001). spin20 spends 103% more, for calls that are 3.2% faster and a p99
  that is 14% lower (both p < 0.001). The energy differs for neither (p > 0.3).
